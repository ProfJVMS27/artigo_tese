# Pre-submission review for *Engineering Applications of Artificial Intelligence*

## 5.1 Summary of the manuscript

This manuscript presents a surrogate-assisted, multi-objective workflow for a prescribed seven-layer barrier intended for a radiological dispersal device response setting. MCNP6.3 photon-transport outputs and LS-DYNA fragment-impact outputs are used to train response-specific surrogates for effective dose and maximum penetration. The study compares raw and physics-guided representations, calibrates split-conformal residual intervals, performs mixed discrete-continuous evolutionary search, benchmarks NSGA-II against NSGA-III and bounded-archive random search at equal evaluation budgets, and regenerates thirty pre-specified candidates in both solvers. The manuscript's most useful contribution is not a new learning algorithm. It is the transparent connection between a coupled engineering design problem, response-specific surrogate limitations, and solver re-evaluation of optimizer-selected candidates.

## 5.2 Overall assessment

The work is a credible fit for an engineering-AI journal. It addresses a concrete, computationally expensive engineering decision problem, uses established AI methods with a sufficiently broad tuned benchmark, and does not misrepresent NSGA-II as a new metaheuristic. The manuscript also has unusual strengths: it reports unfavorable validation behavior, does not overclaim the conformal screen, distinguishes in-domain solver re-evaluation from physical validation, and limits the application claim to substantial supported barriers.

However, it is **not ready for submission to EAAI**. Two submission-facing issues are material. First, the title contains the undefined acronym ``RDD,'' and the abstract contains several undefined abbreviations. Second, the data-and-code statement explicitly says that public deposition remains to be completed. EAAI's stated scope and desk-screening conditions make both issues important, particularly the public reproducibility package. My pre-submission recommendation is therefore **Major Revision before submission**, not because the scientific core is unsound, but because these conditions are remediable and should be resolved before the manuscript enters editorial screening.

## 5.3 Major comments

1. **Resolve title and abstract acronym compliance before submission.**

   The current title ends with ``RDD response.'' Because a title provides no prior definition, this conflicts with EAAI's stated rule against undefined acronyms in titles and abstracts. The title should spell out the term: *Uncertainty-aware surrogate-assisted multi-objective design of hybrid ballistic-radiological shields for radiological dispersal device response.* The abstract defines RDD at first use, but still uses MCNP6.3, NSGA-II, CV, RMSE, MAE, and RAW without expansion. At a minimum, expand the solver name or identify it as a code, write ``Nondominated Sorting Genetic Algorithm II (NSGA-II),'' and replace or expand the performance abbreviations on first use. This is an editorial-screening risk, not a stylistic preference.

2. **Public reproducibility materials are required before submission.**

   The present data-availability statement says that materials are local and that public deposition is a future author action. That is transparent, but it is not a submission-ready availability statement for a journal that explicitly emphasizes public data for replicability. Before submission, deposit a curated repository containing the processed dose and penetration datasets, split identifiers, feature definitions, tuning summaries, frozen surrogate bundles or a reproducible training path, optimization manifests, validation tables, source hashes, scripts, software environment specification, and a README explaining what cannot be redistributed because of commercial solver licensing. A GitHub repository archived through Zenodo, with a versioned DOI, is an appropriate route. Do not upload solver executables or any asset that cannot legally be redistributed.

3. **The engineering scope is defensible, but it must remain narrow.**

   The 95--256 kg solutions are not wearable shields, and the manuscript correctly says so. Maintain that discipline throughout the title, abstract, highlights, cover letter, graphical abstract, and submission metadata. The defensible application is computational screening of substantial, supported barrier concepts under the modeled gamma--fragment scenario. It is not certification of first-responder body armor, an operational RDD-response protocol, or general protection against all radiological and ballistic threats.

4. **The paper has in-domain solver re-evaluation, not external generalization or physical validation.**

   The thirty regenerated cases are a meaningful high-fidelity check of selected numerical candidates, but they are generated by the same two numerical-model families that supplied the learning labels. They do not establish transport to another source geometry, material library, fragment spectrum, or experimental setting. The manuscript already acknowledges this limitation. Retain that language, and avoid calling the thirty cases ``independent validation'' without the qualifier ``independent solver re-evaluation'' or an equivalent precise formulation.

5. **The numerical-impact limitation is properly disclosed, but it remains a reviewer risk.**

   The case-9012 refinement diagnostic does not establish mesh convergence. It instead reveals coupled sensitivity to discretization, contact, and erosion. Reporting this is the correct choice. It must not be softened into a convergence claim, and the newly added Supplementary Table S4 should remain in the submission package. A reviewer may still ask for a re-formulated and revalidated impact model. That request cannot be answered by reinterpreting the current diagnostic. The appropriate response is that such model development is outside the frozen-model scope and that all claims are restricted accordingly.

6. **The operational model-selection rationale is acceptable but deliberately limited.**

   The paper correctly states that the 35 tuned candidate pipelines were ranked using nonnested development CV, then frozen before calibration and testing. This is appropriate transparency. Do not describe the training-CV ranking as an unbiased estimate of model-selection performance. The manuscript should retain the conditional bootstrap wording and the statement that XGBoost's narrow penetration-CV advantage over HistGB is not a population-level superiority claim.

7. **Verify that the generative-AI declaration reflects actual use.**

   The manuscript includes the required declaration, which is a strength. The authors, rather than an editing tool, must confirm that its wording accurately describes every substantive AI-assisted use in manuscript preparation. If an AI system materially reorganized text, drafted substantive prose, or created or altered any figure, the statement should name the relevant service and purpose, and figure captions should disclose any AI-assisted figure generation. The declaration must be accurate, not merely minimal.

## 5.4 Minor comments

1. The abstract is informative but dense. Expanding first-use abbreviations while remaining within the journal's word limit will improve both compliance and readability.
2. ``RAW'' should be styled as ``raw-input'' in the abstract unless it is explicitly defined there. Its uppercase styling otherwise reads as an unexplained acronym.
3. Figure 5 combines two results. The paragraph after the figure thoroughly interprets the solver-based objective-space panel, but the original NSGA-II convergence panel should receive one explicit sentence of interpretation in the immediately following discussion, even if it is only to state that it summarizes ten original trajectories and does not establish global convergence.
4. The data-availability statement should be replaced only after a public repository and DOI exist. Do not insert a placeholder DOI.
5. Retain the distinction between a physical radiation source model and a blast model. The current captions and scope statements do this well.

## 5.6 Recommendation

**Major Revision before submission.** The manuscript has a publishable engineering-AI core and substantially better methodological transparency than a typical applied surrogate-optimization submission. Nonetheless, the undefined-acronym issue and the absence of a public reproducibility deposit create avoidable EAAI desk-screening risk. Address those items, make the one figure-panel clarification, and verify the accuracy of the AI-use declaration. At that point, the manuscript can be submitted with an appropriately cautious cover letter.
